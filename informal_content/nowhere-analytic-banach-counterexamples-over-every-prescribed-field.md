# Nowhere-analytic Banach counterexamples over every prescribed field

For every nontrivially normed field K of characteristic p>0 and every natural k≥p, there exist actual K-Banach spaces E and F, with E'=E, such that for every finite index type ι with card ι=k and every f₀∈L_K(E,E), AnalyticAt K Q^K_{E,E,F} f₀ is false. Equivalently no bounded k-linear lift exists, and ContDiffAt K ω Q f₀ fails everywhere. The same F has no equivalent ultrametric norm. The witnesses are E=E₁ completed-tensor-π Khat and F=B completed-tensor-π Khat, where K₁⊆Khat is the closure of F_p(t), 0<‖t‖<1, E₁=ℓ∞(ℕ,K₁), and B is its degree-k projective exterior completion.

## Proof

Take t∈K with norm strictly between 0 and 1 and form the normed-field completion Khat. Its characteristic remains p because K embeds injectively, and it is complete, nontrivially normed and ultrametric. The Laurent-subfield theorem identifies the closed K₁⊆Khat isometrically with F_p((X)) of norm parameter ‖t‖; K₁ is spherically complete. Transport the Laurent counterexample to K₁. The completed projective tensor construction gives concrete Khat-Banach spaces E,F. A lift over Khat would descend to the prohibited lift over K₁, so none exists. Regard E,F as K-Banach spaces. A bounded K-lift would upgrade by scalar density to a Khat-lift, again impossible. The all-base-point lift criterion gives nowhere analyticity, and finite-index transport handles every ι of cardinality k with the same E,F. Since k≥p≥2, the target-growth argument and the isometric embedding B→F prove that F has no equivalent ultrametric norm. No spherical-completeness assumption on the prescribed K is made.
