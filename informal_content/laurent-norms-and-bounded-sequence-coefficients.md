# Laurent norms and bounded-sequence coefficients

For κ a field and 0<r<1, give K₁=κ((X)) the norm ‖a‖=r^ord(a) for a≠0. This makes it a complete nontrivially normed ultrametric field. Put E₁=ℓ∞(ℕ,K₁) and E₀=κ^ℕ embedded as constant Laurent sequences. E₁ is Banach and ultrametric; nonzero elements of E₀ have norm 1. For nonzero x∈E₁ there is an attained least coordinate order ν(x) with ‖x‖=r^ν(x), and its coefficient sequences x_[n] vanish for n<ν(x). Coefficient zero is κ-linear and vanishes on bounded arrays of sup norm <1. Finally M_r=max_{l∈ℕ}(l+1)r^l exists and is positive; for r≤1/2 it equals 1.

## Proof

Compose the integer valuation with n↦r^n and prove the norm axioms using multiplicativity and the minimum-order rule for sums. The induced topology is the Laurent valuation topology, so completeness transports. Constants have order zero. Sup-boundedness implies that the integer orders of all nonzero coordinates have a common lower bound; a nonempty lower-bounded subset of Z has a minimum, attained at one coordinate, giving the sup formula. Norm<1 forces all orders at least 1, so coefficient zero vanishes coordinatewise. The sequence (l+1)r^l tends to 0 and its initial value is 1, hence its positive supremum is achieved in a finite initial segment. If r≤1/2, (l+1)r^l≤(l+1)/2^l≤1 by induction.
