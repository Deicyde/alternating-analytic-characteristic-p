# Discrete-distance completeness implies spherical completeness

Let Y be a complete ultrametric space whose distances belong to r^Z∪{0} for some 0<r<1. Then every nonempty family of pairwise intersecting nonempty closed balls has nonempty total intersection; equivalently Y is spherically complete.

## Proof

Replace each nonnegative ball radius by the largest r-power not exceeding it, allowing radius zero; the ball is unchanged because distances are discrete. Any two intersecting ultrametric balls are nested according to radius. If the family of effective radii has a least member, that ball lies in all the others and any of its points is common. Otherwise all radii are positive and have infimum zero. Choose a strictly decreasing sequence of radii tending to zero and corresponding balls. Their centers form a Cauchy sequence because the balls are nested. Its limit lies in each chosen closed ball. Every original ball contains some chosen ball, so it contains the limit.
